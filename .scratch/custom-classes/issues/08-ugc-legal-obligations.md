# What must a small EU-hosted hobby site do when hosting user-published content?

Type: research
Status: resolved

## Question

Primary sources only (EU Digital Services Act text, Polish implementing law, official guidance). What notice-and-action mechanism is required, what applies to copyright claims, what the obligations are on discovering CSAM or other illegal content (reporting, preservation, who to notify), and which obligations a micro/small provider is exempt from. Output feeds the moderation decisions.

## Answer

- DSA applies: public Custom Classes make scvmrack a hosting service and online platform. As a micro provider it is exempt from Arts. 15 and 20–28 (no formal complaint system, ODS, trusted flaggers or transparency reports), except Art. 24(3): give user numbers when asked.
- Still mandatory: an Art. 16 report mechanism usable by **anyone** (including signed-out users and rightsholders), capturing explanation + URL + name/email (optional for CSAM) + good-faith statement. Plus receipt and decision emails to the notifier, and an Art. 17 statement of reasons to the author on every takedown.
- Also mandatory: public contact points for authorities and users (Arts. 11–12, PL + EN), terms that explain moderation (Art. 14), and acting on authority orders (Arts. 9–10; TCO 1-hour removal orders).
- Copyright: DSA notice-and-action is enough; scvmrack isn't a DSM Art. 17 "online content-sharing service provider" (profit-organised). CSAM or threats to life: report promptly to Polish Police or prosecutor (DSA Art. 18; Penal Code Art. 240 for listed offences), with Dyżurnet.pl as an extra channel. Hide, don't delete, keep a moderation log, and keep classes text-only.
- Uncertain: whether a donation-funded hobby site is covered at all, and the status of Poland's DSC act (UKE, reportedly signed about 25 Sep 2026, not yet in force). EUR-Lex and gov.pl were unreachable, so the texts were read from verbatim GitHub mirrors.
